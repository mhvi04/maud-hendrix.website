Your parcel has not arrived or an order is stuck. You look for a phone number or an email address and find a search bar and a chat instead. It feels like a detour, but that is how Vinted has set it up officially.

The official Vinted support page lists four entry points. They are the Help Centre, help through your order, a contact button at the end of many articles and a chat with a virtual assistant. There is no phone number or email address on it.

Below is what each entry point does and the order in which to use them.

This article follows the Vinted support page as it stood online on 20 October 2026. Vinted can change how the app works.

## Start with the Help Centre

The Help Centre is the first stop for general questions. Vinted itself recommends starting there. If you type a question in the search bar while logged in, you get an answer that AI has summarised from Help Centre articles.

That answer is not a conversation. If you need help with your own situation, you have to contact support afterwards.

Many articles end with a ‘Contact us’ button. It usually sits at the bottom of the page. Press it and follow the on-screen steps to send a help request to the support team.

## If you have a problem with an order, start in the order

Orders have their own entry point. You go to your profile and open My orders. On the order you press the ⓘ icon and choose Help.

The Help Centre homepage also lists your recent orders. Pick one and you get the articles that match the status of that order.

In the conversation with the other member there is an ‘I have an issue’ button. Vinted uses it to share all order details with support automatically.

For a lost or damaged parcel you use the same button. You must report within two calendar days, as explained in [your parcel is lost or damaged on Vinted](/en/articles/vinted-package-lost-or-damaged).

**My advice:** with an order, always begin in the order itself. Vinted writes that support can then help you faster because the order details come along automatically.

## First a virtual assistant, then a human

According to Vinted the support chat starts with a virtual assistant. That is an AI bot that draws on Help Centre articles.

Vinted calls it the fastest way to personalised help with common issues. You are told when you are talking to the assistant.

You can ask to be transferred to a human support specialist at any time. According to Vinted the wait for a human is usually longer than for the assistant.

Vinted also writes that the assistant sometimes makes mistakes. If the answer does not fit your situation, ask for a human.

## What the official page does not say

The page has no phone number. It has no email address, no opening hours and no response time.

Third-party sites sometimes list addresses and response times, but Vinted does not confirm them on its own page. I do not repeat them here.

How long support takes to reply in practice cannot be read from that page.

## If your account is blocked

With a blocked account you can still use the website to contact customer support, according to the terms. The appeal runs through the link in the statement of reasons you received. How that works is covered in [your Vinted account is blocked, what now](/en/articles/vinted-account-blocked).

## In one sentence

Start in the Help Centre or in your order. Talk to the virtual assistant first and ask for a human when its answer does not fit. Vinted names no phone number and no response time.

> If you have a problem with an order, open the ‘I have an issue’ button and do not start with a general help request.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Vinted have a phone number?

The official support page lists no phone number. Vinted points to the Help Centre, help through your order, a contact button in articles and the chat.

### Can you talk to a real person at Vinted?

Yes. You can ask for a human in the chat at any time. According to Vinted the wait is usually longer than with the virtual assistant.

### How long does it take Vinted to reply?

The official page names no response time. It only says that a human usually takes longer than the assistant.
