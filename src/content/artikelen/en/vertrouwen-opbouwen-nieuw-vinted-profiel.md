Everyone starts at zero. No reviews, no sales, no reason for a buyer to trust you over the dozens of other sellers listing the exact same item. That's the real starting position of a new Vinted profile, and there's no point denying it.

But it's not a permanent state either. Trust on Vinted builds up in layers, and some of those layers build faster than you'd think.

## Layer 1: your profile itself

The first layer is your profile itself. A fully filled-out bio, a clear profile photo and a short text about how you sell. Not inspirational, but informative. Something like "I sell vintage Ralph Lauren and ship within 24 hours" is more concrete and credible than an empty page. Buyers who are on the fence click into profiles to scan whether someone looks real and active. That first impression partly decides whether they ask a question or click away.

## Layer 2: response speed

The second layer is response speed. In my data across more than 2,500 transactions, one pattern is consistent: replying within the hour to a question or offer gives you 40% more chance of actually closing that transaction. For a new profile this matters even more, because fast communication is the only direct signal a buyer has of how serious you are. A buyer's confirmation in your review section starts with that first interaction — the same response speed that also plays into [accepting offers](/en/articles/biedingen-accepteren-op-vinted).

## Layer 3: the first reviews themselves

The third layer is the first reviews themselves. Those first ten are the hardest and the most important. A strategy I see working with new sellers is to deliberately price their first items sharply, not at a loss but at the lower end of the market price. The goal isn't maximum margin on those first transactions, it's maximum speed. Sell fast, ship fast, and then proactively invite the buyer to leave a review. Repeat buyers give an average score of 4.95 out of 5 in my data. But there has to be a first purchase before that.

## Layer 4: packaging, the first physical proof

A fourth element beginners underestimate is packaging. In the review texts I've collected over the years, words like "neatly packaged" and "beautifully wrapped" show up significantly more often in positive reviews from new buyers. The unboxing is the first physical proof of how seriously you take this. Tidy packaging, a little note, and clothing that arrives clean and folded, those are the things that motivate a new buyer to leave five stars instead of four.

## What you sell matters too

Finally, what you sell matters too. Brands buyers recognise and trust lower the purchase threshold on an unfamiliar profile. If Ralph Lauren is on the tag, the buyer knows what they're getting, regardless of whether you have twenty reviews or zero. That's one of the reasons beginning resellers often get traction faster with recognisable branded items than with generic or unfamiliar pieces.

> Going from zero to ten reviews is the slowest phase. After that, it moves faster than you'd think.
