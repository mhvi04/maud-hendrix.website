You're looking for a discount code for Vinted. That code doesn't exist, not for second-hand listings from private sellers. Vinted itself runs platform-wide promotions now and then (free shipping, reduced service fees), but a code that gets you 20 percent off one specific item from one specific seller isn't out there waiting to be found. It's not a secret nobody shared. It simply doesn't exist.

What does exist, and what works better than any code ever could, is negotiating. I've been selling on Vinted for years and have over 2,500 transactions behind me at this point. I've received about as many offers as items sold, and there's a clear pattern in that. Some offers I accept within a minute. Others I ignore. The difference is never the percentage someone is asking for. It's how they ask.

## Why bidding is the real discount

On Vinted, the asking price is rarely fixed. Most sellers price with some margin built in, precisely because they expect negotiation. Making an offer isn't something to do quietly or apologize for. It's just how the platform works. The problem is that most buyers approach it the wrong way and end up missing what was actually possible.

## What doesn't work

A message that just says "lowest price?" is the fastest way to get ignored. It gives the seller nothing to respond to. No amount, no reason, no context. Most sellers either let that sit or answer with the full price, simply because there's nothing to actually negotiate over.

The same goes for an offer that's way under the asking price with no explanation. Asking for fifty percent off on an item that's been listed for five minutes doesn't feel like negotiating to a seller, it feels like someone testing whether they're paying attention. That backfires.

And bidding on something that was just listed is usually wasted effort. In the first few days a listing still has full visibility, and the seller feels no pressure to budge.

## What does work

**A concrete number.** Not "can you do better on the price", but "would 18 including shipping work?". That gives the seller something to respond to directly: yes, no, or a counter. A concrete figure gets answered roughly three times more often than a vague question.

**Timing.** An item that's been listed for three to four weeks with no interest is far more negotiable than something just posted. Sellers see the declining visibility on a listing that's been sitting, and they're more willing to move on price. It's usually easy to check how long something has been up, it's right there on the listing.

**Bundling.** Ask for a discount on one single item and the margin for the seller is thin. Ask for a discount on two or three items together and it becomes worth it for them, because they can ship once instead of three separate times. That's a real saving they feel, and they'll often pass some of it on. How a seller looks at this from their side is covered in [bundle discounts on Vinted](/en/articles/bundle-discounts-on-vinted).

**End of month.** Some sellers are doing this as a side income and want to move stock or close out their monthly numbers by month's end. Not everyone, but it's not a bad time to try an offer.

**A short, human tone.** "Hey, nice piece. Would you consider 22 including shipping?" consistently works better than a cold, bare offer. Sellers respond faster and more positively to messages that sound like there's an actual person on the other end.

## How far can you go

A realistic offer usually sits somewhere between ten and twenty percent below the asking price, higher on items that have been listed a while or on bundles.

| Situation | Realistic offer |
| --- | --- |
| Item just listed | Little room, better to wait |
| Item listed for three to four weeks | Ten to twenty percent, closer to the upper end |
| Bundle of multiple items | Ten to twenty percent off the total, closer to the upper end |

Go systematically lower than that and you risk sellers just ignoring your offer outright. Negotiating works because it gives both sides something. The moment it stops making sense for the seller, the conversation stops too. Why sellers almost never accept a first offer straight away is covered in [what my sales data shows about offers](/en/articles/accepting-offers-on-vinted).

## Short example

Asking price: 28, listed for three weeks.

Bad message: "Discount possible?"

Good message: "Hey, lovely piece. Noticed it's been up a while, would you consider 22? Can pay right away."

The second message gives a number, acknowledges the item has been sitting, and closes with something that reassures the seller: a quick, certain sale.

## Conclusion

There's no code. There's a system, and that system is making offers in a way that gives the seller something back in exchange for the discount you're asking for. A concrete number, the right moment, a human tone. That works better than any discount code you were never going to find anyway.

> No code, just a system: offer a concrete amount, pick a moment when the item has been listed for weeks and keep your message short and human. Aim for ten to twenty percent below the asking price and ideally ask for a discount on multiple items at once.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a discount code for Vinted?

Not for second-hand listings from private sellers. Vinted itself runs platform-wide promotions now and then, such as free shipping or reduced service fees, but a code that gets you 20 percent off one specific item from one specific seller doesn't exist.

### How do you get a discount on Vinted?

By negotiating in a way that gives the seller something in return. Offer a concrete amount instead of asking for the lowest price, pick a good moment, ask for a discount on multiple items at once and keep your message short and human.

### How much discount can you ask for on Vinted?

A realistic offer usually sits between ten and twenty percent below the asking price, higher on items that have been listed a while or on bundles. Go systematically lower and sellers will simply ignore your offer.

### When is the best time to make an offer on Vinted?

On an item that has been listed for three to four weeks with no interest, because the seller sees the declining visibility and is more willing to move on price. Bidding on something that was just listed is usually wasted effort. The end of the month can also work with sellers who use Vinted as a side income.

### What should you not send when asking for a discount on Vinted?

A message that just says "lowest price?" or "discount possible?". It gives the seller no amount, reason or context to respond to, so it usually gets ignored. The same goes for an offer far below the asking price with no explanation.
