A buyer has favourited two or three of your items and asks whether you can knock a bit off if they take everything together. The question sounds harmless, but the answer directly touches your margin, your average order value and, if you're not careful, your own bookkeeping. Bundling is a strong tool, but only if you know in advance how much discount is reasonable and where the hidden costs sit.

## Why bundling is worth it

Bundling first and foremost raises your average order value. Two items that cost 47 and 35 euros separately, you sell together for, say, 77 euros. The buyer feels like they scored a deal, and you sell in one transaction what would otherwise have been two separate, slower sales. It's also a strong tool for items that have been sitting online for a while without traction: instead of just lowering the price or relisting, you can pair that item with a faster-moving piece to keep it circulating.

Bundling also works particularly well with a specific type of buyer. Buyers who browse a lot, are price-sensitive and regularly make low offers respond noticeably well to a bundle proposal, even when they'd never have bitten on an individual item at full price. Where on a single item you don't want to go further than your usual negotiation floor — the same floor that comes into play when [accepting offers](/en/articles/biedingen-accepteren-op-vinted/) — a bundle gives you more room precisely because the total amount still comes out higher for you than a single sale would.

## The rule of thumb: 15 to 25 percent

In practice a healthy bundle discount moves between 15 and 25 percent below the sum of the individual asking prices.

| Number of items | Typical bundle discount |
| --- | --- |
| 2 to 3 items | 10 to 15 percent |
| 4 or more items | Closer to 20 percent |

With two to three items you usually sit at the lower end of that range, around 10 to 15 percent. When a buyer asks for four or more items at once, a discount closer to 20 percent is more common, because the buyer is also taking more volume off your hands, and it clears more stock for you in one go.

It matters that the discount applies to the total, not to each item separately. Two cable knit sweaters at 47.50 euros each, that's 95 euros combined, might become, say, 80 to 85 euros for the bundle, a discount of roughly 11 to 16 percent on the total. That already feels like an attractive step to the buyer, while it still leaves you a healthy margin on each individual piece.

## The floor: never below your absolute minimum

However attractive a bundle feels, there's a hard line you should never let erode. Make sure every basic item in the bundle still leaves an absolute margin of at least 10 to 15 euros after the discount. A T-shirt you bought for 6 euros and normally sell for 25 can absolutely give up a slice of its margin in a bundle, but not so much that barely anything is left after purchase cost and shipping. A bundle discount is meant to sell faster and raise your average order value, not to give away margin just because a buyer asks for it.

The same discipline applies to your overall price positioning. If you structurally go too far below the median price for your category, even within bundles, you start undermining your own positioning as a reliable, quality reseller over time. An occasional sharp bundle deal is fine, a habit of always discounting heavily the moment someone asks for a bundle undermines what you can ask for your individual items in the long run.

## What a bundle costs beyond the discount itself

There are a few cost items in bundle sales that sellers often overlook, and that affect your final margin without you immediately noticing. Shipping costs for a large bundle can add up considerably for the buyer, sometimes up to 8 to 17 euros depending on weight and destination. That often pushes the buyer to negotiate even harder on the price of the clothing itself, to stay within their total budget — the same anchoring effect that also shows up in [the hidden costs of Vinted](/en/articles/verborgen-kosten-van-vinted/). Keep that in mind when you propose a bundle price: don't just calculate the discount on the clothing, but also what the buyer ends up paying net, shipping included.

There's also an administrative trap in bundle sales that easily leads to a wrong profit estimate. If in your own records you assign the full bundle price to each individual item instead of splitting that price proportionally, you systematically overstate your profit. That's not a theoretical risk, it's a mistake that happens in practice and can noticeably distort your yearly figures if you repeat it across multiple bundles. So always split the bundle price proportionally across the individual items in your bookkeeping, never the full price multiple times.

Finally, bundle sales carry a slightly higher risk of cancellations and incomplete transactions than a single sale. While a buyer is still selecting items or negotiating the bundle price, those items are in practice somewhat less visible to other interested buyers. If the bundle then falls through anyway, you've spent time on communication for nothing in return. That's not a reason to never bundle, but it is a reason not to wait endlessly on a hesitant buyer while there's also interest in the individual items in the meantime.

## How you frame it

The tone in which you offer a bundle makes a difference in how much a buyer is willing to pay. Offering a discount as something the buyer has earned themselves works better than a discount that feels like caving to negotiation pressure. Something like a short, friendly confirmation that your bundle discount applies once someone takes multiple items communicates that it's a fixed, fair policy, not the result of pushing. That preserves the perceived value of your listings, even though you do end up giving a discount. That same logic also works the other way around: a [favourite on multiple items](/en/articles/favoriet-hartje-op-vinted/) is itself often already the signal to proactively come with a bundle proposal, rather than waiting for the buyer to ask for one.

> Plan on a bundle discount of 15 to 25 percent on the total, with the smaller discounts for two to three items and the larger ones for four or more. Always protect an absolute minimum margin per item, even within a bundle, and don't lose sight of your overall price positioning.
