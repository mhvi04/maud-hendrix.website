You buy an item listed in a currency other than your own, and without really noticing, you pay an extra percentage on top of the price. It's not prominently advertised, but it's explicitly written into Vinted's terms. Here's what it is and when it actually hits you.

## What currency conversion on Vinted is

If an item is listed in a currency different from the one you normally pay in, Vinted offers a service that lets you check out in your own currency anyway. That service isn't provided by Vinted itself, but by their Payment Processors or Vinted Wallet Providers. They convert the amount into your currency at the moment you pay.

## What it actually costs you

For that conversion, a fee of either 1.2 percent or 3 percent of the item's price is charged. Which percentage applies depends on exactly which currencies are involved. This comes on top of the regular Vinted fee and the item price itself.

## Where and when this applies

This service isn't available everywhere, it's only offered in specific countries. If currency conversion is available in your registered country and you buy an item priced in a different currency, this fee is automatically charged the moment you pay. You really only notice it at checkout, not earlier in the buying process.

## Can you avoid it

Barely. In theory you could only buy items already listed in your own currency, but that obviously limits what you can find quite a bit, especially with international sellers or specific brands that mostly sell in one region. For most buyers, this is simply a cost that comes with shopping cross-border on Vinted.

## Why this is worth knowing about

Most buyers have no idea this exists until they see the amount at checkout and wonder why the price is suddenly higher than expected. It's not an error and not a hidden trick in any dishonest sense, it's just not prominently stated on the listing itself. If you regularly buy or sell internationally on Vinted, this is a cost worth factoring into your budget, especially on larger purchases where 3 percent quickly adds up to a few extra euros.

## The short version

Buy an item in a currency other than your own, and you may be charged an extra 1.2 to 3 percent, depending on the currencies involved and whether the service is available in your country. Check this on larger international purchases before you hit pay.
