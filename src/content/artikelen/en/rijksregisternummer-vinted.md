You open the Vinted app and a notice asks you to fill in a form, including your national register number (rijksregisternummer). You have done nothing wrong, yet it feels like a second-hand app is asking for something it should not. That reaction is fair.

The answer is less dramatic than the notice suggests. Vinted asks for the number because a European directive requires it, and the Belgian tax administration (FOD Financiën) uses it to identify you with certainty. It is an identification number, not a tax bill.

That does not mean it changes nothing. Your details do reach the tax office, and if you do not submit the form you lose access to selling and to your Vinted Wallet.

## What Vinted has to pass on

On its own page about DAC7, the FOD Financiën explains that platforms must pass your details to it in most cases. This follows from the European directive DAC7. The FOD lists five types of information.

| Item | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Identification | Your identification details, including your national register number |
| Account | The bank account where you receive payments, and the account holder if that is not you |
| Transactions | The number of transactions completed |
| Income | The income you received |
| Costs | All costs, taxes or commissions charged to you |

Your national register number is therefore one item in a larger package. The FOD's list contains counts and amounts, not individual items. The tax statements with this information are in your personal file on MyMinfin, under Mijn documenten.

## When this applies to you

For the sale of goods, the FOD says the obligation only applies if you earn more than €2,000 and/or make 30 or more sales per year and per platform. Either one is enough. Vinted's own DAC7 page names the same threshold: at least 30 sales or more than €2,000 per calendar year.

The threshold applies per platform. The VRT reports that the same rules apply to 2dehands, eBay and Airbnb, so sales on another platform do not add to your Vinted total.

One caveat: the VRT writes at least €2,000, while the FOD writes more than €2,000. I follow the FOD, because it is the source of the rule.

Above that threshold you get reported. Whether you also pay tax is a separate question — see [the Vinted exemption](/en/articles/de-vinted-vrijstelling) — and the calculation is in [paying tax on Vinted sales in Belgium](/en/articles/belasting-betalen-op-vinted-in-belgie).

## Why the national register number and not just your name

Many users wonder why they cannot give this to the FOD directly. The VRT put that question to the FOD in February 2025. According to the FOD, it has no view of who holds an account on Vinted, because only the platform knows who sold how much.

A name alone is not enough, the FOD says. Some people share a name, and only the unique national register number gives certainty that it is the right person. Platforms must pass the data to the authorities of the user's country.

## Is it safe?

The fact that the number is unique is both its strength and its weakness. According to the VRT, people with bad intentions can misuse the number together with a name and other details for identity fraud.

The FOD cannot check itself how platforms handle that data. Other bodies do. Platforms must respect the GDPR, use the data only for tax purposes and secure it well, or they risk a fine.

Vinted says the data is encrypted with AES-256, that you receive a copy of what you submit and that the information goes to the authorities the following year. That is a promise from the platform and not a check by the FOD.

## What happens if you do not submit the form

Vinted is clear about this. If you do not submit the form, you can no longer sell and you lose access to your Vinted Wallet. You get that access back at any time once you submit the form.

Most members have to submit it by early January. In some cases you get more time. The VRT wrote in 2025 that Vinted applies the European rules unusually strictly and that users who do not provide their details are temporarily blocked.

I did not find an official source for the exact date the restriction starts. If your account is stuck for another reason, see [your account is blocked on Vinted, what now](/en/articles/account-geblokkeerd-op-vinted).

## If you are a Pro seller

This article assumes a private seller. The FOD names the national register number as part of your identification details. For a Pro account you also need a company number (ondernemingsnummer). I did not find an official source stating exactly which details Vinted asks Pro sellers for under DAC7.

## What to do

**My advice:** count your sales and your turnover for this year. If you reach 30 sales or more than €2,000, the request will come.

- Submit the form by early January. It is pre-filled and Vinted asks you to add one detail.
- Check that what it shows is correct before you confirm, including the bank account.
- Keep the copy Vinted sends you.
- Later, check on MyMinfin, under Mijn documenten, that your tax statement matches what you tracked yourself.

## The shortest summary

Vinted asks for your national register number because a European directive requires it and because the FOD Financiën would otherwise not know for sure who you are. It applies from 30 sales or more than €2,000 per year on Vinted. It is not a tax bill, but if you do not submit the form you can no longer sell and cannot reach your Wallet until it is sorted.

> Submit the form by early January and check your tax statement on MyMinfin afterwards.

### Do I have to give Vinted my national register number?

If you pass 30 sales or €2,000, yes. Platforms are required to collect it, and Vinted restricts your access until you submit the form.

### Do I pay tax because Vinted passes on my details?

No, reporting is not an assessment. Vinted itself says that meeting the criteria does not mean you owe tax. Whether you are taxed depends on other rules, as explained above.

### What if I do not want to give my number?

Then you can no longer sell on Vinted and cannot reach your Wallet. You get access back as soon as you submit the form.
